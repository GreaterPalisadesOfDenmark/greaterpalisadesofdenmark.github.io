Monthly Questionnaire: October 2025

  1. Does God lead us into temptation? (Mt 6:13; Lk. 11:4)
    • GPD Translation: “And allow us not to fall into temptation…”
    • Comment: God does not lead a man into temptation, but “allows” him to fall into temptation for reasons known only to Him.
  2. Has Jesus come to set a man against his father? (Mt. 10:35)
    • GPD Translation: “For I have come to separate a man from his father…”
    • Comment: What ever happened to the Fifth Commandment? Christ came only to separate the believers from the unbelievers.
  3. Was the Holy Virgin ever a wife? (Lk. 2:5)
    • GPD Translation: “To be registered with Mariam, the betrothed to him woman, being expectant.”
    • Comment: Nine months after the Annunciation, when they went to Bethlehem, they were still engaged.
  4. Does the original text say “her firstborn son”? (Mt. 1:25; Lk. 2:7)
    • GPD Translation: “And was not knowing her until she brought forth her son the firstborn…”
    • Comment: This verse has been tainted to promote the idea that the Lady of the Theotokos, our Holy Mother, had other children.
  5. Is the Theotokos “highly favored”? (Lk. 1:28)
    • GPD Translation: “Rejoice, thou full of grace, the Lord is with thee…”
    • Comment: The Theotokos is very graceful, above the Cherubim and the Seraphim.
  6. Is vain repetitions correct? (Mt. 6:7)
    • GPD Translation: “But when you pray, do not babble incoherently, as the heathen do…”
    • Comment: The original Greek refers here to “incoherent babbling”.
  7. Who is this Matthat fellow in the English translation? (Lk. 3:23,24)
    • GPD Translation: “…the son of Joseph, who was the son of Heli, who was of Matthan, who was of Levi…”
    • Comment: The end “t” in “Matthat” reveals that Christ’s genealogical list in Lk. 2:23 confused the KJV scholars, so they changed the name so as not to have to explain. Matthat is the same person as Matthan in the genealogical list in the first chapter of Matthew; he is the grandfather of both Joseph and Mariam according to St. Nikdoemos of the Holy Mountain.
  8. Why leave out “this is true indeed”? (Lk. 11:28)
    • GPD Translation: “This is true indeed, blessed are they that hear the word of God, and keep it.”
    • Comment: The word that defines “this is true indeed” is “μενοῦνγε”. Even the NKJV has it right in Romans 10:18. But here in Luke 11:28, it has been omitted. Why is this? Is it because “it is true indeed” sheds some light on the exalted person of the Theotokos.
  9. Where is “born again” found? (Jn. 3:7)
    • GPD Translation: “Marvel not that I said to thee, you must be born from above.”
    • Comment: “Born again” is not found in the Bible, but fabricated for some reason. The ancient text says: “born from above”.
  10. Why do several English translations omit the word fasting? (Mk. 9:29)
    • GPD Translation: “And He said to them, This kind can come out by nothing but prayer and fasting.”
    • Comment: No comment.
  11. Is it right to use the phrase “out of his mind”? (Mk. 3:21)
    • GPD Translation: “And when His friends heard of it, they went out to lay hold of Him: for they said, He went into ecstasy.”
    • Comment: The word “ἐξέστη” in the original text means “ecstasy”.
  12. Is it correct to use the phrase “do not resist an evil person”? (Mt. 5:39)
    • GPD Translation: “But I say to you, Do not resist the evil one…”
    • Comment: “πονηρῷ” in Holy Scripture is ascribed to the devil unless it is explicitly stated otherwise. The word “person” is an add in.
